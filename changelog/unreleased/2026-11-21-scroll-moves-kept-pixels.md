- **A scroll redraws the strip that came in, not the window, on Linux and in headless runs.** The frame Rune keeps for partial redraws holds a scrolled viewport rolled, so a scroll moves none of its pixels: the frame copies the window once (as every partial frame does), redraws what came in, what changed and what stayed put over what moved, and copies only that back. Rows cut by the viewport's edge and rows that came into the list or left it no longer make the frame draw whole. A frame that does draw whole draws into the kept frame, so the one after it is partial again; a redrawn rectangle is cleared by writing one colour, skips the root's fill, and draws only the instances that reach it; a frame whose instances didn't change draws no pass. Every frame holds the bytes it held drawn whole (`RUNE_WHOLE_FRAMES=1`). On an M1 Pro, GPU time per frame of an app's scripted scrolls fell 33 to 60%; a scripted scroll runs 3 to 19% more CPU instructions, the planning and the extra copies and draws. The frame log (`RUNE_FRAME_LOG`) gained `gpu_wait_ms`, the wall time from a frame's submit until the GPU had drawn it.
