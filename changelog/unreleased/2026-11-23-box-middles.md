- A big box (a rect 96 device pixels or more each way, not banded, with no per-side border or fade stops in its clip) and a radial gradient's stop segment draw as their middle and four bands around it, split in the vertex stage (`SPLIT_VERTICES`): the middle is where the shape and its clip cover every pixel whole, and a box's middle draws with a small pipeline that returns its fill (`variant::solid_wgsl`, made on a thread from WGSL where the uber shader is; the uber shader draws it until then, and elsewhere). An outline with nothing inside it draws as bands, as a border with no fill does. The uber shader skips coverage and clip deep inside a box or a clip. No pixel changes. On a small GPU (Intel's 24-EU UHD) a bar's settings window took 40% less GPU time a frame, its sheets 10 to 30% less; a software rasteriser's time fell by a third. The vertex stage changed, so the shipped Metal and DX12 bytecode is stale until regenerated (`regen_dxbc`, the metallib build): those platforms compile the WGSL meanwhile.
- The pipelines pinned to one kind are built from WGSL on Vulkan and GL by default: on that GPU they took a fifth off a bar's GPU time for a few percent more CPU. `RUNE_PINNED_WGSL=0` turns them off.
